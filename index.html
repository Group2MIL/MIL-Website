ABOUT US

Who We Are

Learning, reflecting, and creating change.

The Initiative for Human and Environmental Wellbeing is a student-centered project created to explore issues that affect people and the world around us.

Our initiative focuses on three interconnected areas: Health, Education, and Environment. These areas influence our everyday lives and the choices we make as individuals and as members of society.

Through our essays and autobiographies, we share research, personal reflections, and experiences that encourage readers to think about their own role in creating a healthier, more educated, and environmentally responsible community.

RATIONALE

Why This Initiative?

The Initiative for Human and Environmental Wellbeing was developed from the idea that meaningful change often begins with awareness and small everyday decisions.

Health affects our ability to learn, work, build relationships, and pursue our goals. Education gives people the knowledge and skills needed to understand the world and adapt to change. Meanwhile, the environment provides the resources that allow communities to live and grow.

These three areas are closely connected. A healthy person needs a healthy environment. Education helps people understand how their choices affect their health and surroundings. Environmental responsibility protects the resources that future generations will depend on.

This initiative therefore aims to encourage students to look beyond academic requirements and recognize how their everyday decisions can contribute to the wellbeing of themselves, their communities, and the world around them.

“ Change begins when awareness becomes action. ”

HEALTH

Health Is Wealth: How Simple Habits Can Improve Our Everyday Lives

01

HEALTH

Taking Care of Our Physical Health

Health is one of the most important parts of our lives. There is a reason people say that health is wealth because no amount of money or material success can completely replace good health once it is lost. Being healthy is not only about being free from diseases or illnesses. It also means taking care of our bodies through the choices we make every day.

As students, we often become busy with classes, assignments, examinations, projects, and other responsibilities. Because of this, taking care of our physical health can sometimes become something we forget about.

One of the most important parts of physical health is proper nutrition. Food provides the energy and nutrients our bodies need to grow, function, and remain healthy. According to the World Health Organization, a healthy diet should have adequacy, balance, moderation, and diversity.

We can start with simple habits such as eating regular meals, drinking enough water, and including fruits, vegetables, whole grains, and sources of protein in our diet. We do not have to completely remove our favorite foods from our lives. Instead, we can learn how to enjoy them in moderation.

Physical activity is another important part of maintaining a healthy body. Walking, dancing, playing sports, riding a bicycle, swimming, stretching, and even doing household chores can help us stay active.

Sleep is another part of physical health that students sometimes ignore. Teenagers generally need 8 to 10 hours of sleep each day. Getting enough sleep can support concentration, behavior, mental health, and academic performance.

Personal hygiene is also important. Bathing regularly, washing our hands, brushing our teeth, wearing clean clothes, and keeping our surroundings clean are simple habits that can help protect us from illness.

Taking care of our physical health does not mean that we have to be perfect. What matters is consistency. Eating properly, staying active, getting enough sleep, and practicing good hygiene are simple choices that can have lasting effects.

02

HEALTH

More Than What We Can See

When people talk about health, they usually think about the physical body. We think about eating healthy food, exercising, avoiding sickness, and maintaining good hygiene. However, health is not only something that can be seen.

A person can look perfectly healthy on the outside while struggling with stress, anxiety, sadness, or emotional exhaustion. This is why mental and emotional health should be treated as an important part of our overall well-being.

Students experience different kinds of pressure every day. We have assignments to finish, examinations to prepare for, projects to complete, and expectations that we try to meet. Aside from school, we may also experience family problems, friendship issues, financial concerns, and worries about the future.

According to the World Health Organization, one in seven adolescents between the ages of 10 and 19 experiences a mental health condition. Anxiety, depression, and behavioral disorders are among common concerns among adolescents.

These facts show why mental health should not be ignored. Feeling stressed, worried, sad, or overwhelmed does not automatically mean that someone is weak. Difficult emotions are a normal part of life.

One way to protect our mental health is by giving ourselves time to rest. Taking short breaks between school tasks, listening to music, spending time with people we care about, or doing hobbies can give our minds time to recover.

Another important part of mental health is having people we can trust. When problems become too difficult to handle alone, talking to a friend, family member, teacher, counselor, or another trusted person can provide support.

Health is more than what can be seen in a mirror. Sometimes, the struggles that require the most attention are the ones that cannot be seen at all.

03

HEALTH

Health in Our Daily Lives

Good health does not usually come from one major decision. More often, it is built through the small choices we make every day. The food we eat, the amount of physical activity we get, how much we sleep, and how we handle stress can all affect our well-being.

One of the easiest places to begin is with our eating habits. Students often have busy mornings and may skip breakfast because they are late for school. Others may choose fast food because it is convenient.

Physical activity is another habit that students should include in their daily routines. Walking, playing sports, dancing, cycling, swimming, stretching, and household chores can all provide opportunities to move.

Sleep is another habit that students often sacrifice. Assignments, examinations, projects, social media, and entertainment can cause us to stay awake later than we should. Teenagers generally need 8 to 10 hours of sleep each day.

Our mental well-being should also be included in our daily routine. Taking time to rest, listen to music, enjoy a hobby, spend time with friends or family, or simply step away from schoolwork can help create a healthier balance.

Knowing when to ask for help is also an important healthy habit. Asking for help does not mean that we are incapable. If we are struggling with schoolwork, stress, relationships, or personal problems, reaching out to someone we trust can make difficult situations easier to handle.

Healthy habits do not need to be complicated. Small and consistent choices are often more realistic and easier to maintain. Taking care of ourselves is something we should begin now.

EDUCATION

Knowledge Is Free and Collectable, But Education Is Not

Education is one of the main ways people gain knowledge, develop skills, and understand the world around them. It is more than simply memorizing information for an examination.

Education is the process of learning ideas, developing abilities, understanding values, and using what we learn in real situations. Knowledge, on the other hand, is what we gain from the lessons and experiences we encounter throughout our lives.

Schools are one of the most common places where education takes place. However, education does not begin and end inside a classroom. UNESCO describes education as a fundamental human right and emphasizes its importance in building peaceful, sustainable, and inclusive societies.

Learning can happen in different ways. A person can learn by reading books, observing other people, asking questions, practicing a skill, or experiencing something personally.

Curiosity is another important part of education. People naturally ask questions about the world around them. Education gives people opportunities to explore these questions instead of simply accepting that they will never know the answers.

Education also helps people understand that not everything they encounter is automatically true. Learning how to analyze information, compare sources, ask questions, and use evidence is especially important in a world where information can spread quickly.

In the end, education is not simply about collecting information. It is about developing the ability to understand, question, and apply knowledge.

02

EDUCATION

Education and Opportunity

Education is often described as a basic part of growing up, but not everyone receives the same opportunities to experience it.

Schools provide a structured environment where students can develop knowledge and skills. Teachers guide students through different subjects, while classmates provide opportunities to communicate and work with others.

However, access to education is not always equal. Poverty, conflict, disability, discrimination, location, and social circumstances can create barriers to education.

This makes education more than simply a personal responsibility. A student cannot be expected to succeed in school if they are not given the opportunity to attend or if the conditions around them make education difficult to access.

Education can also provide people with opportunities beyond what they currently know. A child may enter school with only a basic understanding of the world but eventually discover an interest in science, art, law, business, technology, or another field.

Education should therefore be understood as both a right and an opportunity. Every child deserves the chance to learn, regardless of their financial situation or background.

03

EDUCATION

Education and Adaptation

The world is constantly changing. New technologies are being developed, scientific discoveries are being made, jobs are changing, and societies continue to develop.

Because of these changes, people cannot simply depend on the knowledge that was available in the past. We need to continue learning so that we can understand and adapt to the world around us.

Education is not only about memorizing information that will appear on a test. It is also about developing the ability to understand new information and respond to situations we have never experienced before.

Technology is one clear example of how quickly the world can change. New devices, software, artificial intelligence, and online platforms continue to develop. This means that people need to continue developing their knowledge.

Education can also help people become more critical when dealing with information. The internet gives us access to an enormous amount of information, but not everything online is accurate.

The world will continue changing whether we are ready for it or not. We cannot predict everything that the future will bring, but we can prepare ourselves to keep learning.

How Simple Habits Can Protect Our Everyday World

01

ENVIRONMENT

Our Everyday Impact

The environment is connected to almost everything we do. The air we breathe, the water we drink, the food we eat, and the places where we live all depend on a healthy planet.

One of the simplest ways to help the environment is to become more aware of what we buy and what we throw away. Modern life has made it very easy to purchase things whenever we want them.

The things we buy require natural resources to produce. Materials need to be collected, processed, transported, and eventually disposed of.

We can become more responsible consumers. Before buying something, we can ask ourselves whether we actually need it. We can choose products that can be reused instead of disposable ones.

Plastic waste is also a common problem in our daily lives. Students can reduce their dependence on single-use plastics by bringing reusable utensils, containers, and water bottles.

Protecting the environment does not require us to completely change our lifestyle overnight. It begins with being more aware of our choices.

02

ENVIRONMENT

Using What We Have Responsibly

The environment provides us with many things that we depend on every day. We need water to drink, electricity to power our homes and schools, and transportation to help us travel.

Water is one of the most valuable resources we have. Saving water does not require major changes. We can turn off the faucet when we are not using it, take shorter showers, and fix leaking pipes or faucets.

Energy is another resource that we often use without thinking about it. Lights, fans, air conditioners, computers, and other appliances require electricity.

Students can help conserve energy by turning off lights when leaving a room, switching off computers after schoolwork, and using natural light when possible.

Transportation is another part of everyday life that affects the environment. Whenever it is practical and safe, walking or riding a bicycle can be an environmentally friendly option.

Using resources responsibly is not about making life uncomfortable. It is about avoiding unnecessary waste and understanding the value of the resources we use.

03

ENVIRONMENT

From Concern to Action

Environmental problems have become impossible to ignore. News about climate change, pollution, deforestation, extreme weather, and the loss of wildlife appears regularly on our screens.

Young people already experience pressure from school, family expectations, friendships, and concerns about their future. Adding environmental problems to these worries can sometimes feel overwhelming.

One way to turn concern into action is by participating in environmental activities in our schools and communities. Joining clean-up drives, planting trees, helping with recycling programs, or participating in environmental campaigns can give students the opportunity to contribute.

Spending time in nature is another way to develop a stronger connection with the environment. Walking in a park, visiting a beach, taking care of plants, or helping in a school garden can remind us that nature is something we are physically connected to.

Environmental protection also requires cooperation. Individuals cannot solve global problems alone. Governments, businesses, schools, communities, and young people all have important roles.

We may not be able to change everything, but we can still change something. Sometimes, that is where meaningful change begins.
