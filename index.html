EDUCATION / WRITTEN WORK

Knowledge Is Free and Collectable, but Education Is Not

Education is the intentional process of sharing knowledge, building skills, and shaping values. Knowledge is the collection of lessons a person gains throughout their educational journey. In simpler terms, education is the process through which a person gains knowledge and develops an understanding of the world around them.

Education is commonly received through schools, where knowledge and learning are organized and provided in one place. Going to school has become a standard and an important part of growing up for people in every generation. However, not everyone is fortunate enough to have the money or opportunity to attend school. This does not mean that a person is unable or not allowed to learn. Knowledge can still be collected through reading, observing, and experiencing life.

This can be seen in Kara David's program, “Malasakit,” where she helped raise funds and donations for children living in poverty so they could continue their education. Their situation shows that while education may require resources and opportunities, the desire to learn can exist regardless of a person's circumstances.

In philosophy, learning can also be connected to Rationalism and Empiricism. Rationalism focuses on gaining knowledge through reason and logic, while Empiricism emphasizes knowledge gained through experience and observation. Both show that people can gain knowledge in different ways. Even without enough money to attend school, a person can still read books, observe their surroundings, and learn from their own experiences.

From a child's perspective, there are also basic things that need to be learned, such as:

  • Walking
  • Socializing
  • Counting
  • Reading
  • Learning
  • Understanding values
  • And many more

These are basic skills that children need as they grow. Learning these skills allows them to adapt to their surroundings and prepares them to learn more as they become adults. Education, therefore, does not begin and end inside a classroom. It starts from the earliest stages of life and continues as a person grows.

Why Is Education Important?

Going to school can be a long and difficult experience. Students spend many years studying, completing requirements, taking examinations, and preparing for their future. Because of this, some students may question the purpose of school and wonder if education is really necessary. Some might argue that many things have already been discovered, that not everyone plans to become someone important, or that everyone will eventually die anyway.

However, these arguments overlook the fact that human needs go beyond simply surviving. This can be connected to Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs, particularly its foundation, the physiological needs. These include basic necessities such as food, air, sleep, and clothing that allow a person to live comfortably and physically survive.

Although these needs are essential, they do not complete a person's needs by themselves. Humans also have emotional, social, and intellectual needs. Having modern technology that makes survival easier does not mean that education is no longer necessary. Education allows people to understand the world, make informed decisions, solve problems, and develop beyond their basic needs.

As the world continues to change and improve compared to past centuries, people must also learn how to adapt to their changing environment. This is one of the reasons education is important. There will always be new problems to solve, new discoveries to make, and new situations that people must understand. Someone may discover a cure, develop a new invention, or find a solution to a problem simply because they had the knowledge and curiosity to explore it.

The world is constantly rising and evolving. But how can a person learn to adapt? How can someone know whether something they are touching is dangerous? How can a person satisfy their curiosity and understand the things happening around them?

Education provides knowledge, and knowledge gives people the ability to satisfy their curiosity.

Curiosity is something that cannot simply be removed from the human mind. People naturally ask questions because we live in a world full of uncertainty and possibilities. We want to know how things work, why things happen, and what can be done to make things better.

That is why education exists. It gives people a foundation of knowledge from the beginning of their lives and provides them with the tools to continue learning. Knowledge may be free and collectable, but education provides the opportunity, guidance, and foundation needed to turn that knowledge into something meaningful.

HEALTH / WRITTEN WORK

Health Is Wealth: How Simple Habits Can Improve Our Everyday Lives

Health is one of the most important parts of our lives. There is a saying that our health is our wealth, no amount of money or material success can replace good health or buy it back once it is lost.

It is not only about being disease-free but also about taking care of our physical, mental, and emotional well-being. As students, we often become busy with school activities, assignments, examinations, and other responsibilities.

Because of this, we sometimes forget to take care of ourselves. We skip meals, sleep late, spend too much time on our phones, or ignore stress. Our everyday habits can affect our health in the present and in the future.

Eating nutritious food is one of the simplest ways to maintain good health. Our body needs different nutrients to grow, have energy, and function properly.

The World Health Organization recommends eating a variety of foods, including fruits, vegetables, whole grains, and sources of protein.

It is also important to limit foods that contain too much sugar, salt, and unhealthy fats. Eating healthy does not mean that we can never enjoy our favorite foods. Instead, it means learning how to balance what we eat and making healthier choices most of the time.

Physical activity is another important part of a healthy lifestyle. Exercise can help strengthen our muscles and bones, improve our fitness, and support our mental health.

Physical activity does not always have to be a serious workout. Walking, dancing, playing sports, riding a bicycle, or even doing household chores can help us stay active.

Students experience different kinds of pressure, such as academic responsibilities, family problems, friendships, and personal struggles. Sometimes, we may feel overwhelmed or emotionally tired.

Taking care of our mental health is equally important. Talking to a trusted friend, family member, teacher, or professional can help us cope with difficult situations.

Getting enough rest is also essential. Sleep allows our bodies and minds to recover after a long day.

Good health begins with small and consistent choices. We do not need to completely change our lifestyle overnight.

Eating nutritious food, staying physically active, getting enough sleep, managing stress, and asking for help are simple steps that can make a difference.

We should remember that taking care of our health is just as important as achieving good grades. Our health supports everything we want to accomplish in life.

By caring for ourselves today, we are also preparing ourselves for a healthier future.

ENVIRONMENT / WRITTEN WORK

How Simple Habits Can Protect Our Everyday World

The environment is basically the foundation of everything we do. There is a lot of truth to the old idea that our planet is our ultimate bank account.

No amount of money or success can patch up a broken ecosystem, and you cannot buy back clean air and fresh water once they are gone.

Keeping the planet healthy isn't just about stopping massive ecological disasters either. It is about how we treat our neighborhoods every single day.

As students, it is so easy to get completely buried under classes, assignments, and trying to have a social life.

When you are rushing around like that, you sort of forget how connected we are to everything around us.

We grab single-use plastics because we are in a hurry, leave lights on, or throw away things that still work.

But these little everyday habits add up fast, and they shape the exact world we have to live in right now and down the road.

Watching what we buy and throw away is one of the easiest ways to start looking out for the planet.

Earth only has so many resources to go around, and it needs them to stay balanced so wildlife can survive.

Moving toward a lifestyle where we reuse things and cut down on trash is an important way to reduce pollution.

Saving energy and water is another huge part of living a green life.

When we use less, we help slow down climate change, preserve wild areas, and protect animals.

You do not need a massive lifestyle makeover to make a dent. Just turn off your computer when you are done, shut off the faucet while brushing your teeth, take quicker showers, or take the bus and carpool with friends instead of driving alone.

Being a student right now brings a ton of pressure, from fighting for good grades to feeling genuinely stressed out about the future of the world.

Feeling completely overwhelmed by huge environmental issues is a totally normal thing to go through.

That is why it helps so much to turn that stress into small, local actions.

Joining a campus cleanup crew, planting trees, or even just venting about these worries with friends and teachers can make you feel less helpless because you are working on shared solutions.

We also need to pay attention to how much time we spend around nature.

Spending a little time outside helps us appreciate the world we are trying to save, and it gives your brain a much-needed break.

Fixing the planet starts with small, daily choices.
