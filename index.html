01 / HEALTH

Health Is Wealth: How Simple Habits Can Improve Our Everyday Lives

HEALTH

Taking Care of Our Physical Health

Health is one of the most important parts of our lives. There is a reason people say that health is wealth because no amount of money or material success can completely replace good health once it is lost. Being healthy is not only about being free from diseases or illnesses. It also means taking care of our bodies through the choices we make every day. As students, we often become busy with classes, assignments, examinations, projects, and other responsibilities. Because of this, taking care of our physical health can sometimes become something we forget about.

One of the most important parts of physical health is proper nutrition. Food provides the energy and nutrients our bodies need to grow, function, and remain healthy. According to the World Health Organization (WHO, 2026), a healthy diet should have adequacy, balance, moderation, and diversity. It recommends eating a variety of nutritious foods while limiting foods that contain excessive amounts of unhealthy fats, free sugars, and sodium. The organization also explains that healthy eating habits developed early in life can continue into adulthood.

For students, eating properly can affect how we function throughout the day. It can be difficult to concentrate in class or participate in activities when we are hungry or do not have enough energy. UNICEF (n.d.) identifies unhealthy diets and inactive lifestyles as some of the important health issues affecting adolescents today. This shows why developing healthier habits while we are young is important instead of waiting until we become adults.

We can start with simple habits such as eating regular meals, drinking enough water, and including fruits, vegetables, whole grains, and sources of protein in our diet. We do not have to completely remove our favorite foods from our lives. Instead, we can learn how to enjoy them in moderation while making nutritious food a regular part of our meals.

Physical activity is another important part of maintaining a healthy body. Many students spend several hours sitting in classrooms, studying, using computers, or scrolling through their phones. Although these activities are part of modern life, spending too much time being inactive can affect our health. According to the World Health Organization (WHO, 2024), regular physical activity can improve physical fitness, bone health, cognitive development, and mental health among children and adolescents.

The WHO recommends that children and adolescents get an average of at least 60 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous physical activity each day. However, exercise does not always have to mean going to a gym or following a difficult workout routine. Walking, dancing, playing sports, riding a bicycle, swimming, stretching, and even doing household chores can help us stay active. The important thing is to find activities that we enjoy and make movement part of our routine.

Sleep is another part of physical health that students sometimes ignore. Assignments, examinations, social media, games, and other responsibilities can cause us to stay awake later than we should. However, not getting enough sleep can affect our concentration, behavior, mental health, and academic performance. The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC, 2024) states that teenagers aged 13–18 generally need 8 to 10 hours of sleep each day and that insufficient sleep can contribute to health and attention problems among students.

Personal hygiene is also important. Bathing regularly, washing our hands, brushing our teeth, wearing clean clothes, and keeping our surroundings clean are simple habits that can help protect us from illness. These actions may seem ordinary, but they are part of being responsible for our own health.

Taking care of our physical health does not mean that we have to be perfect. There will be days when we eat our favorite unhealthy food, fail to exercise, or stay up later than we should. What matters is that these choices do not become permanent habits. Health is built through consistency rather than perfection.

Our bodies allow us to study, work, spend time with people we love, and pursue our goals. Because of this, we should not wait until we become sick before we start taking care of ourselves. Eating properly, staying active, getting enough sleep, and practicing good hygiene are simple choices, but they can have lasting effects.

In the end, physical health is built through the habits we practice every day. The choices we make today may seem small, but they can influence our health as we grow older. Taking care of our bodies now is one of the simplest ways we can prepare ourselves for a healthier future.

More Than What We Can See

When people talk about health, they usually think about the physical body. We think about eating healthy food, exercising, avoiding sickness, and maintaining good hygiene. However, health is not only something that can be seen. A person can look perfectly healthy on the outside while struggling with stress, anxiety, sadness, or emotional exhaustion. This is why mental and emotional health should be treated as an important part of our overall well-being.

Students experience different kinds of pressure every day. We have assignments to finish, examinations to prepare for, projects to complete, and expectations that we try to meet. Aside from school, we may also experience family problems, friendship issues, financial concerns, and worries about the future. When several problems happen at the same time, it can become difficult to manage everything.

According to the World Health Organization (WHO, 2025), one in seven adolescents between the ages of 10 and 19 experiences a mental health condition. Anxiety, depression, and behavioral disorders are among the common mental health concerns among adolescents.

These facts show why mental health should not be ignored. Feeling stressed, worried, sad, or overwhelmed does not automatically mean that someone is weak. Difficult emotions are a normal part of life. What matters is learning how to recognize these emotions and finding healthy ways to deal with them.

One way to protect our mental health is by giving ourselves time to rest. Students sometimes feel guilty for taking breaks because they believe they should always be productive. However, constantly working without giving ourselves time to recover can make us even more exhausted.

Physical activity can also support mental health. According to the World Health Organization (WHO, 2024), regular physical activity provides significant physical and mental health benefits. For children and adolescents, it can support cognitive development, mental health, and overall well-being.

Another important part of mental health is having people we can trust. When problems become too difficult to handle alone, talking to a friend, family member, teacher, counselor, or another trusted person can provide support.

We should also learn to recognize when other people may be struggling. Not everyone expresses their emotions openly. Someone who seems happy may be dealing with problems that we know nothing about.

Mental health can also affect education. Anxiety and depressive disorders can affect school attendance and schoolwork, according to WHO (2025). This shows that mental health and academic life are connected.

Social media is another factor that can influence young people's well-being. We constantly see other people's achievements, appearances, relationships, and lifestyles online. Comparing ourselves to what we see online can make us feel as though we are not doing enough.

Health is more than what can be seen in a mirror. Sometimes, the struggles that require the most attention are the ones that cannot be seen at all. By taking mental health seriously, being kinder to ourselves, and supporting the people around us, we can create a healthier environment for everyone.

Health in Our Daily Lives

Good health does not usually come from one major decision. More often, it is built through the small choices we make every day. The food we eat, the amount of physical activity we get, how much we sleep, and how we handle stress can all affect our well-being.

One of the easiest places to begin is with our eating habits. Students often have busy mornings and may skip breakfast because they are late for school. Others may choose fast food because it is convenient or forget to eat properly because they are focused on schoolwork.

Physical activity is another habit that students should include in their daily routines. Schoolwork and technology can cause us to spend many hours sitting. According to WHO (2024), regular physical activity can improve physical fitness, cognitive development, mental health, and overall well-being.

Sleep is another habit that students often sacrifice. Assignments, examinations, projects, social media, and entertainment can cause us to stay awake later than we should. According to the CDC (2024), teenagers between 13 and 18 years old should generally get 8 to 10 hours of sleep per day.

Because of this, managing our time is part of maintaining our health. Finishing schoolwork is important, but constantly sacrificing sleep and rest can make it harder for us to function properly.

Our mental well-being should also be included in our daily routine. School can be stressful, especially when several deadlines and examinations happen at the same time.

This is why students should learn to recognize when they need a break. Taking time to rest, listen to music, enjoy a hobby, spend time with friends or family, or simply step away from schoolwork for a while does not mean that we are avoiding our responsibilities.

Knowing when to ask for help is also an important healthy habit. If we are struggling with schoolwork, stress, relationships, or personal problems, reaching out to someone we trust can make difficult situations easier to handle.

Healthy habits do not need to be complicated. We do not have to completely change our lifestyle in one day. Small and consistent choices are often more realistic and easier to maintain.

As students, we sometimes think that getting good grades is the most important thing we need to achieve. However, our health should not be sacrificed just to meet academic expectations.

In the end, a healthy lifestyle is built through everyday decisions. The choices we make today may seem small, but they can become habits that influence our future.

02 / EDUCATION

Knowledge is free and collectable, but education is not.

LEARN

Education and Knowledge

Education is one of the main ways people gain knowledge, develop skills, and understand the world around them. It is more than simply memorizing information for an examination. Education is the process of learning ideas, developing abilities, understanding values, and using what we learn in real situations.

Schools are one of the most common places where education takes place. Students spend years attending classes, completing activities, reading books, conducting research, and learning from teachers. However, education does not begin and end inside a classroom.

Unfortunately, not everyone has equal access to education. Some children are unable to attend school because of poverty, conflict, distance, discrimination, or other circumstances. According to UNICEF (n.d.), millions of children and young people around the world remain out of school.

Learning can happen in different ways. A person can learn by reading books, observing other people, asking questions, practicing a skill, or experiencing something personally. Rationalism gives an important role to reason and knowledge derived through thought, while Empiricism emphasizes experience and observation as sources of knowledge.

Even basic activities involve learning. A child learns how to walk, communicate, socialize, count, read, write, follow rules, and understand values. These abilities provide a foundation for more complicated learning later in life.

Curiosity is another important part of education. People naturally ask questions about the world around them. Education gives people opportunities to explore these questions instead of simply accepting that they will never know the answers.

Reading is one way people can continue learning even outside formal education. Books, articles, research, and other reliable materials allow people to encounter ideas that they may never experience personally.

Education also helps people understand that not everything they encounter is automatically true. Learning how to analyze information, compare sources, ask questions, and use evidence is especially important in a world where information can spread quickly.

Education therefore provides more than facts. It gives people tools for understanding those facts. It allows us to ask questions, examine evidence, learn from experience, and develop our own understanding.

The world continues to change, and the knowledge people need also changes with it. New technologies, discoveries, careers, and problems continue to appear. Because of this, learning should not stop when a person leaves school.

In the end, education is not simply about collecting information. It is about developing the ability to understand, question, and apply knowledge.

Education and Opportunity

Education is often described as a basic part of growing up, but not everyone receives the same opportunities to experience it. Education therefore involves not only learning but also access to opportunities that can influence a person's future.

Schools provide a structured environment where students can develop knowledge and skills. Teachers guide students through different subjects, while classmates provide opportunities to communicate and work with others.

However, access to education is not always equal. Poverty remains one of the factors that can prevent children from receiving education. Some families may struggle to afford school supplies, transportation, uniforms, technology, or other expenses connected to schooling.

Programs that help children receive education can therefore make an important difference. One example is Project Malasakit, which works to support communities and children through different forms of assistance.

Education can also provide people with opportunities beyond what they currently know. A child may enter school with only a basic understanding of the world but eventually discover an interest in science, art, law, business, technology, or another field.

Learning also happens outside school. A person can learn skills from their family, community, work, books, personal experiences, or other people.

Education can also influence a person's ability to participate in society. A person who receives quality education may have more opportunities to pursue further studies, employment, or other goals.

Not every student will have the same interests, talents, or goals. Some may want to become doctors, lawyers, teachers, engineers, artists, entrepreneurs, or something completely different.

In the end, education should be understood as both a right and an opportunity. Every child deserves the chance to learn, regardless of their financial situation or background.

Education and Adaptation

The world is constantly changing. New technologies are being developed, scientific discoveries are being made, jobs are changing, and societies continue to develop. Because of these changes, people cannot simply depend on the knowledge that was available in the past.

Education is not only about memorizing information that will appear on a test. It is also about developing the ability to understand new information and respond to situations we have never experienced before.

Technology is one clear example of how quickly the world can change. New devices, software, artificial intelligence, and online platforms continue to develop.

Education also helps people understand risks. In everyday life, we encounter things that can be dangerous if we do not understand them. We learn about chemicals, electricity, diseases, natural disasters, technology, and other potential risks through education and experience.

Curiosity plays an important role in this process. People naturally want to know how and why things work. Education provides opportunities for people to explore their curiosity.

A person may graduate from school, but they will continue to encounter new situations throughout life. Continuing to learn allows people to adapt instead of becoming completely dependent on what they already know.

Education can also help people become more critical when dealing with information. The internet gives us access to an enormous amount of information, but not everything online is accurate.

The importance of education can also be connected to Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs. Maslow's theory suggests that people have different levels of needs, beginning with basic physiological needs and extending toward safety, belonging, esteem, and self-actualization.

This helps explain why education matters beyond basic survival. Humans do not only want to survive. We also want to understand, create, discover, improve, and accomplish things.

Education therefore gives people more than knowledge for the present. It gives them the ability to gain knowledge for the future.

In the end, education remains important because the world is never finished changing. What we know today may not be enough for the problems of tomorrow.

03 / ENVIRONMENT

How Simple Habits Can Protect Our Everyday World

EARTH

Our Everyday Impact

The environment is connected to almost everything we do. The air we breathe, the water we drink, the food we eat, and the places where we live all depend on a healthy planet. Because of this, protecting the environment should not only be the responsibility of governments, organizations, or environmental groups.

One of the simplest ways to help the environment is to become more aware of what we buy and what we throw away. Modern life has made it very easy to purchase things whenever we want them.

The things we buy require natural resources to produce. Materials need to be collected, processed, transported, and eventually disposed of. When we constantly purchase unnecessary products and throw them away after a short period of time, we contribute to the growing amount of waste.

We can become more responsible consumers. Before buying something, we can ask ourselves whether we actually need it or whether we simply want it because it is popular. We can choose products that can be reused instead of disposable ones.

Fast fashion is another issue worth considering. Trends change quickly, and social media often encourages people to constantly buy new clothes to keep up with them.

Plastic waste is also a common problem in our daily lives. Single-use plastics are convenient because they are easy to use and throw away, but their convenience can come with environmental consequences.

Our habits at school can also affect the environment. Students use paper, electricity, food packaging, and other materials every day.

It is easy to believe that one person's actions are too small to make a difference. However, environmental problems are often created by millions of small actions happening repeatedly.

Protecting the environment begins with being more aware of our choices. Every time we reuse something instead of throwing it away, refuse unnecessary plastic, or think carefully before buying something, we are making a small decision that can contribute to a bigger change.

By becoming more responsible about what we consume and what we throw away, we are not only reducing waste but also protecting the resources that future generations will need.

Using What We Have Responsibly

The environment provides us with many things that we depend on every day. We need water to drink, electricity to power our homes and schools, and transportation to help us travel from one place to another.

Water is one of the most valuable resources we have. We use it for drinking, cooking, cleaning, bathing, and many other activities.

Saving water does not require major changes. We can turn off the faucet when we are not using it, take shorter showers, fix leaking pipes or faucets, and avoid using more water than necessary.

Energy is another resource that we often use without thinking about it. Lights, fans, air conditioners, computers, televisions, and other appliances require electricity.

Students can help conserve energy through simple actions. Turning off lights when leaving a room, switching off computers after finishing schoolwork, unplugging devices when they are no longer needed, and using natural light when possible can reduce unnecessary electricity use.

Transportation is another part of everyday life that affects the environment. Many vehicles depend on fossil fuels, which contribute to air pollution and greenhouse gas emissions.

Whenever it is practical and safe, walking or riding a bicycle can be an environmentally friendly option for shorter distances.

Of course, not everyone has the same transportation options. Some students live far from school, while others may not have access to public transportation or safe areas for walking or cycling.

Using resources responsibly is not about making life uncomfortable. It is about avoiding unnecessary waste. We can still use electricity, water, and transportation while being more conscious of how we use them.

The resources we have today will also be needed by people in the future. If we learn to use them carefully now, we can help protect the environment while still meeting our needs.

From Concern to Action

Environmental problems have become impossible to ignore. News about climate change, pollution, deforestation, extreme weather, and the loss of wildlife appears regularly on our screens.

While being aware of these problems is important, constantly thinking about them can also make us feel worried and powerless.

UNESCO (2024) has identified eco-anxiety as a growing concern among young people. Eco-anxiety refers to feelings of worry, fear, or distress related to environmental problems and the future of the planet.

It is understandable to feel concerned when environmental problems seem so large. Climate change and pollution cannot be solved by one student alone.

One way to respond is by participating in environmental activities in our schools and communities. Joining clean-up drives, planting trees, helping with recycling programs, or participating in environmental campaigns can give students the opportunity to contribute directly.

Talking about environmental concerns can also help. Students can discuss environmental issues with their classmates, teachers, families, and communities.

Spending time in nature is another way to develop a stronger connection with the environment. Walking in a park, visiting a beach, taking care of plants, or helping in a school garden can remind us that nature is not just something we read about in textbooks.

Students can also make small environmental choices in their daily routines. We can bring reusable bottles and bags, reduce unnecessary waste, save water, turn off lights and appliances when they are not needed, and properly dispose of our garbage.

Environmental protection also requires cooperation. Individuals cannot solve global problems alone. Governments need effective policies, businesses need to consider sustainable practices, schools need to educate students, and communities need to work together.

Our generation will eventually inherit the environmental conditions created by the choices made today. This can be frightening, but it can also be a reason to take action.

We cannot personally repair every damaged ecosystem or solve every environmental problem. However, we can choose what we do with the part of the world that is within our reach.

By turning concern into action, spending time in nature, making responsible choices, and encouraging others to participate, we can become part of the solution. We may not be able to change everything, but we can still change something.
